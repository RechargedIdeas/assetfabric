49. **Live channel transport = files** (tickets / HEAD / acks under `.fabric/live/`), not sockets/gRPC — simplest atomic medium that works headless and in CI; a Studio WS/gRPC transport can sit on the same ticket protocol later.
50. **Live-tunable surface** = numeric leaves of `game_data.json` matched by `content/tune/limits.yaml` + `sim.leash` / `sim.separation`; ranges are ours. Structural edits require `forge build`.
51. **Health probe** = 90-tick forward clone + invariants (finite positions, 0 ≤ HP ≤ max, max > 0, casts resolve). Spec says only "health probe".
52. **ACK target ≤ 500 ms and recovery ≤ 10 s** are our soft targets (spec gives none); tracked in `results/F4.json` `perf`.
53. **Guardian policy**: heartbeat = `status.json` mtime (~2 Hz), hang 5 s default (3 s in acceptance), backoff 0.5 s × 2ⁿ capped 8 s, give up after 5 restarts in 120 s. **Poison-ticket quarantine** (in-flight ticket with no ack is never re-applied) is our addition.
54. **Statechart regions** combat / movement / ai / dialogue / reload and their events (`move/stop`, `freeze/resume/swap`, `intent`, `dialogue_request/reply`, reload 2PC events) are ours; SCXML subset without hierarchy/history.
55. **Session snapshot cadence** 300 ticks + after each commit; snapshots are JSON (not binary) for diffability.

## F5 — Monte Carlo balance, soft-lock detection, save migration, first boss

56. **F5-1 Matchup definition**: a G-BAL-01 "matchup" is a character-select choice = leader ∈ 4 base fighters × starter pet ∈ 3 starters (12 cells, GAME_SPEC §6.1 step 1). The other three base fighters follow without pets. GAME_SPEC does not define "matchup".
57. **F5-2 Target band**: mean win rate must sit in 0.55–0.90 (tuning target 0.72) — a boss that everyone beats (or nobody beats) is "balanced" but useless. Spec gives no band.
58. **F5-3 Dice**: balance seeds vary ±20 % damage rolls, 10 % ×1.5 crits and hostile spawn cells; common random numbers (same seed list for every matchup).
59. **F5-4 Save versions**: v1 (flat prototype) and v2 (layered, int levels) model formats we migrate *from*; v3 is the first shipped layout (profile / roster / world, GAME_SPEC §4.5), canonical JSON + sha256 checksum, atomic tmp→rename with `.bak` fallback.
60. **F5-5 XP**: xp per defeated hostile = tier × 10, granted to every party member (placeholder until progression design, D-005).
61. **F5-6 Rally**: the autoplay leader holds just outside an un-pulled tier-3 boss's aggro ring until every living follower is inside the leash. Before this, whoever was picked as leader tanked the opening alone and the balance runner measured pathing order, not the choice.
62. **F5-7 Boss design**: Cave Warden = tier 3 (×3.2 stats), 3 HP-threshold phases (1.0 / 0.66 summon 2 emberlings / 0.33 enrage ×1.25 atk), telegraphed kit cleave / ember_bolt / quake. Names and numbers are ours (D-005).
63. **F5-8 Deviation gate** (revised at F6, see F6-2): G-BAL-01 "< 5 % across matchups" is gated on each *choice* (each leader's and each pet's marginal win rate) **and, from F6, on each leader × pet cell** vs. the mean, measured on ≥ 1000 fights (default 2400). Through F5 the per-cell spread was only reported (12.7 pp) because the sim has strong pairwise interactions; F6 step 0 closed that debt with pet↔character affinity (F6-1).
64. **F5-9 Tuning space**: Optuna TPE (CPU) over boss HP/ATK and the three starter-pet bonuses; tuned on seed_base 50 000, verified on seed_base 1000 (no overfit to the gate seeds). Fighter stats stay as authored.
65. **F5-10 Runtime soft-lock**: no damage or healing for 1200 ticks (20 s) while both sides live = `softlock`; tick limit = `timeout`. Probe: every ability cooldown at the live-tune maximum must be flagged.
66. **F5-11 "5 %" is absolute** (percentage points of win rate), not relative to the mean. Relative reading (5 % of 0.732 = 3.66 pp) also holds for the gated choice deviation (3.04 pp).
67. **F5-12 Tuning evidence**: shipped boss/pet values were picked with `forge balance tune` during development (that run was interrupted before its log was committed); the committed log `results/F5-tune.json` is a short reproducible study (12 trials × 24 seeds) and its best point is *not* applied because 24 seeds/matchup is noise-dominated. Verification at 200 seeds is the authority.
68. **F5-13 Boss music**: the boss fight plays the existing G1-Audio `boss` stem of `music_cave`; no new audio brief.

## F6 — Vertical slice: quests, farming/craft economy, loot, adaptive music, cave ↔ open world

69. **F6-1 Pet↔character affinity**: GAME_SPEC §4.2 says pets have "character affinity"; we model it as a per-(pet, leader) multiplier on the pet's *own-leader* bonus (`pets.yaml` `affinity`, live-tune bounds 0.25–3.0, default 1.0). Values were fitted per cell: response curves at affinity 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.75 / 3.0 on seed_base 5000 (isotonic in log-affinity) → round 1 (hold-out on seed_base 1000: 9.0 pp) → round 2 corrected with that reading at 50 % shrink → **3.21 pp** on the gate seeds (seed_base 1000, partly in-sample) and **5.25 pp** on unseen seed_base 9000. Boss and fighter stats were not retuned — stat bonuses alone could not move the wisp cells (flat response).
70. **F6-2 Per-cell gate and the noise floor**: at 200 seeds per cell the per-cell standard error is ≈ 3.2 pp, so the expected max |deviation| of 12 *perfectly balanced* cells on fresh seeds is ≈ 5–6 pp. The gate is measured on the fixed acceptance seeds (seed_base 1000, CRN) where the fit is partly in-sample; on unseen seeds (9000) the spread is 5.25 pp ≈ the noise floor. Raising seeds to ~800/cell would make an out-of-sample per-cell gate meaningful; not done to keep CI and the 2-core node reasonable.
71. **F6-3 Economy content**: items, crops (grow ticks, yields), recipes, shop prices, start kit and loot tables (`content/world/*.yaml`) are placeholders of ours (D-005 open); only their *shape* comes from GAME_SPEC §4.4 (farming/gather/grow/craft feeds economy and combat) and §5 (loot tied to tiers).
72. **F6-4 XP curve**: per_tier 10 xp per kill (F5-5) with level thresholds 0/30/90/200/360 and starting level 2; level-ups apply the roster's per-level growth on the next area build.
73. **F6-5 Recovery rules**: fallen party members revive at 25 % HP when a fight ends; arriving at the home field fully heals; a wipe reloads the area at the last checkpoint (wipe counter mixed into the seed). Spec does not define death/recovery.
74. **F6-6 Save v4**: World layer gains farm plots, checkpoint (area + arrival edge), quest counters, rng state, world tick, depleted nodes, cleared packs, active buffs; Roster gains unlocked fighters, party HP and gear. Checkpoints are written on every area transition and at the bench (`rest` stage). v3 → v4 migration fills defaults (GDScript ↔ Python parity).
75. **F6-7 QA autopilot**: the headless slice is played by `world/autopilot.gd` — a deterministic scripted agent (no learning, no LLM) that drives the leader through the same `perform()` actions a player uses; combat is the normal BT/policy party. A player-driven leader exploring the world is not pushed by follower separation (followers part around it); otherwise a follower parked on the next waypoint could pin the leader.
76. **F6-8 Quest model**: quests are compiled to one parallel statechart (one region per quest) with event triggers (`select`, `plant`, `gather`, `enter`, `cleared`, `defeated`, `save`, …) and guard expressions over have/total/flag counters (`content/world/quests.yaml`, key `when` — `on` is a YAML 1.1 boolean). Persistent facts (`cleared:<area>`, `defeated:<boss>`) are re-offered after each transition so stage order can never soft-lock; the chart is checked by the F4 static analyzer.
77. **F6-9 Adaptive music**: stems layered by biome (field → `music_field`, caves/tunnels → `music_cave`) and state (explore / tension within 176 px of a hostile / combat / boss) with 45-tick fades; volume maps in `content/world/music.yaml` are ours.
78. **F6-10 Slice route**: field (farm, bench, flint) ⇄ tunnels (2 packs, flint) ⇄ deep cave (3 packs, moss) → Warden's lair (sealed until the craft stage) → open-world exit back to the field. The lair and the open-world exit are gated by quest stage / `warden_down` flag. Area names and layout are ours; GAME_SPEC §4.4 lists caves, tunnels and open world as biomes.
79. **F6-11 Scripted freeze-swap**: the QA script freezes time once per area visit in the boss fight and in the first big (≥ 3 hostiles) deep-cave fight, records the overlay, checks threaded == serial advisor ranking, and swaps to the top recommendation; then control returns to the selected leader after combat.
80. **F6-12 Slice length**: GAME_SPEC §6.1 targets 15–20 min for a human. Measured bot length is ≈ 2.8–3.4 game-minutes (max-efficiency autopilot: no exploration, reading, menus or misplays). The length target is a **soft** metric that needs a GUI playtest; it is not gated.
81. **F6-13 Boss reward**: clearing the Cave Warden unlocks the fifth fighter `vex` (roster unlockable, INFERENCE 28) and drops a tier-3 loot table (`warden_core`); the spec ties unlocks and loot to progression/tiers but does not name the reward. `warden_core` + glowcap craft the leader-unique `warden_charm` (def +1.5) after the fight.
82. **F6-14 Hold-out reporting**: the per-pair gate is evaluated on the fixed acceptance seeds (seed_base 1000); an unseen-seed run (seed_base 9000, 5.25 pp) is reported in F6.md but not gated, because at 200 seeds/cell it is at the statistical noise floor (F6-2). Promoting it to a gate is deferred until ~800 seeds/cell is affordable.

## F7 — Desktop / Android / Web launch candidates, PWAPipeline, ShuttleLine multi, BoltPack

83. **F7-1 Targets and modes**: four targets — `desktop-linux` (x86_64, release), `desktop-windows` (x86_64, release, cross-exported from Linux), `android` (**debug** APK, `arm64-v8a` only for the Redmi Note 12 Pro 5G reference (D-002), Godot 4.3 defaults minSdk 21 / targetSdk 34, versionCode 7 = milestone, versionName 0.7.0), `web` (release, **threads** + PWA). GAME_SPEC §5 asks for "Desktop signed builds"; signing is deferred by brief (unsigned/debug is acceptable for F7) and tracked in docs/packaging.md §Signing.
84. **F7-2 Staged keep-import**: Fabric output is runtime-loaded and hidden from the editor import by `.gdignore` (F1 design), and Godot never exports a `.gdignore`d directory. Rather than changing the game tree (and the determinism gate), packaging copies the project to `dist/.stage/`, removes the `.gdignore` there and writes `importer="keep"` stubs for PNG/WAV so they ship byte-for-byte; ShuttleLine proves it per target.
85. **F7-3 Web threading (D-001)**: we ship the **threads** build (the swap advisor uses `WorkerThreadPool`) with COOP/COEP from the host and Godot's service-worker header shim for hosts that cannot set headers (one automatic reload on first visit). A single-threaded (`nothreads`) variant is not built in F7; its templates are fetched so it can be added if a target host breaks isolation. D-001 stays open for the Factory Lead with this as the proposed default.
86. **F7-4 "45 MB compressed"**: measured as the sum of gzip-9 sizes of every file in `dist/web/` (what a gzip-serving host transfers; brotli would be smaller), against 45 000 000 bytes (decimal MB, the stricter reading).
87. **F7-5 Web save persistence**: Godot persists `user://` to IndexedDB from the main loop after a written file is closed, so quitting in the same frame can drop the save. `Save.quit_after_persist()` / `Save.persist_wait()` wait 10 frames + 0.5 s on Web only before quitting (used by the slice's save-and-quit path and `savecheck`).
88. **F7-6 ShuttleLine "multi"**: SPEC says "Multi-engine export (Godot first; Unity/Unreal adapters) — multi@F7". F7 delivers the engine-neutral **contract** (`shuttleline.contract/1`) verified against every shipped *platform* artifact, plus an engine-neutral `generic-folder` adapter. Unity/Unreal adapters are declared *not built* in the contract (GAME_SPEC ships Godot only; no consumer yet).
89. **F7-7 Script bytes across exports**: Godot re-tokenises GDScript (`.gdc`) per export and the bytes are not stable between exports, so Windows content is compared with Linux by file set + byte-identical data files, not whole-PCK equality. Behavioural equality is proven by running the shipped content (Linux/Android).
90. **F7-8 Android without a device**: no emulator is possible on the build host (no usable `/dev/kvm`). The APK is verified statically (aapt, apksigner, apkanalyzer) and its **packed content** (`assets/` = exported project) is run by the exported desktop runtime — full slice + save hop. Reports label this "Android content on the desktop runtime"; an on-device run (D-002) is TODO.
91. **F7-9 G-SAVE-01 reading**: "Desktop ↔ Android ↔ Web save round-trip" = a save written on one platform loads, rewrites and continues on the others with no data loss. Proven as a ring: desktop export quits mid-slice (checkpoint 4) → Android content imports + rewrites → Web imports + rewrites into IndexedDB and reloads it **offline** → desktop export resumes and ends byte-identical (canonical JSON) to an uninterrupted run; checksum identical at every hop. Transfer between devices is manual (`--import-b64`); cloud saves are out of scope (GAME_SPEC §5).
92. **F7-10 "Networking stack dormant" (G-NET-01)**: Game Zero v1 has no networking code; dormant = the multiplayer API stays on Godot's `OfflineMultiplayerPeer`, no socket/HTTP/WebRTC class is referenced by any game script (static scan), the desktop slice completes inside `bwrap --unshare-net`, the APK requests no `INTERNET` permission, and the Web build boots from the SW cache with the network off. G-NET-02 (P2P + TURN) is later.
93. **F7-11 G-A11Y-01 scope at F7**: the input-remap half is data-level — `profile.settings.input_remap` ({action: [key names]}) is applied at boot/load and travels with the save on every runtime; colour-blind palette variants exist in PatternVault (F1). An in-game rebinding UI and a runtime palette toggle are **not built**, so G-A11Y-01 is reported *partial*.
94. **F7-12 Live channel in exported builds**: exported builds (including the debug APK and Web) never open the F4 live channel implicitly — only the editor/dev runtime (`OS.has_feature("editor")`) or an explicit `--live-dir`. Keeps shipped builds off the file channel.
95. **F7-13 Default scene**: the boot router's default changed from `sandbox` to `slice`, so a shipped build opens the game; all acceptance runs pass `--scene` explicitly except the F7 desktop launch, which deliberately relies on the default.
96. **F7-14 BoltPack policy**: SBOM components for the engine come from the binary's own `Engine.get_copyright_info()` (superset across modules, cached in `.fabric/cache`), not from a hand list. License allow-list = permissive ids + `LicenseRef-Proprietary`; any GPL/LGPL/AGPL/SSPL/EUPL atom fails; MPL-2.0 (Mozilla CA bundle inside the engine) is allowed and reported as weak copyleft. Python build tools are not in the game SBOM. Bolts are **unsigned** until a release key exists.
97. **F7-15 GIRT budget accounting (CI de-flake)**: a policy call over its 500 µs budget still returns scores, so it is now counted as `policy_over_budget`, not a fallback; the F3 gate keeps `policy_fallbacks == 0` and adds **p95 ≤ budget** (measured p95 ≈ 20 µs). Before, a single scheduler hiccup failed F3 ≈ 1 run in 4 on the reference box (also on `main`).
98. **F7-16 CI scope**: platform exports and the full F7 acceptance stay local (≈1 GB templates, JDK + Android SDK, Chrome, ≈ 2.5 min) to keep the ≈ 14 min pytest job from growing; CI runs the F7 fast subset. A cached `package` job is a TODO.
99. **F7-17 "Tier1+Tier2 launches"**: SPEC §13 names the F7 game milestone "Tier1+Tier2 launches" without defining the tiers. Read as Tier 1 = desktop (Linux + Windows, GAME_SPEC §5 "Desktop signed builds"), Tier 2 = Android + Web PWA (GAME_SPEC §6.2 G-WEB-01 / G-SAVE-01). "Launch" = a launch **candidate** build that passes the gates locally; nothing is published (producer direction: no public releases).
100. **F7-18 Windows proof without Windows**: no Windows host or wine on the build box, so the Windows build is accepted on static evidence (PE32+ x86-64 headers, PCK content == Linux, same template set) rather than a runtime smoke; the BUILD.json records `wine_smoke.ran = false`. A Windows run is a TODO before any external hand-off.

## C1–C6 — PocketLoom companion (standalone Android + LoomLink)

SPEC §8 / §13 give C1–C6 one-line focuses ("PocketLoom / FabricCore", "On-device generation", "Standalone UI",
"Local capture", "Pairing (QR/codes/Tailscale)", "Offload + sync") plus the §8 bullet list. Everything below is how
those lines were made concrete.

1. **C-1 FabricCore is JVM-only Kotlin, not Kotlin Multiplatform yet.** SPEC says "Kotlin Multiplatform shared module".
   The module is pure Kotlin with zero third-party runtime dependencies (own JSON, PNG encoder, HMAC via `javax.crypto`),
   so adding KMP targets later is mechanical; today the Android app and a desktop JVM (the `fabriccore-all.jar` CLI used
   by `forge accept C*`) share it. Desktop *Studio* integration is Python (`forge/loomlink.py`), wire-compatible.
2. **C-2 What "C1" means.** C1 = the FabricCore runtime contract (JobSpec/JobExecutor, Dispatcher, AssetStore with
   version vectors, OffloadQueue, Telemetry) + an installable APK shell, verified by unit tests, aapt and apksigner.
3. **C-3 "On-device generation" = LocalForge CPU procedural** (CA caves, mirrored sprites) with the same gate vocabulary
   as desktop F1 generators (solvability, floor ratio, palette lock, symmetry, fill). Gate rejections retry on derived
   sub-seeds `seed·1 000 003 + k` (k ≤ 3) — deterministic; `attempts` recorded. Not byte-identical to the desktop
   generators (different algorithms), only the same contract.
4. **C-4 "small ONNX" is a labelled stub.** No ONNX Runtime is bundled (≈ +10–20 MB per ABI, no trained small models exist
   yet). The backend ladder lists `onnx-small` first and reports it unavailable with the reason; the CPU rung always
   runs (GroundWeave rule: CPU fallback for every ML path). `ModelCache` (byte-budget LRU, pins) is real and tested so the
   ONNX rung can plug in.
5. **C-5 UI toolkit = platform widgets, no AndroidX/Compose**: keeps the APK ≈ 1 MB and the Gradle build offline-friendly.
   Tabs: Generate · Library · Capture · Link · Telemetry ("telemetry UI, generate screen, local asset store" in §8).
   All text in string/plural resources (English only for now; locale files later).
6. **C-6 Capture = stills via the system camera app or import**, not an in-app camera (Camera2/CameraX) — no CAMERA
   permission, no AndroidX. The camera writes through a private non-exported `ContentProvider`. Captures are stored with
   telemetry (source, time, device, size, dimensions) and a `recon.capture` job is queued; the desktop only *stores* the
   capture (reconstruction is C10–C12). Video/burst/ARCore depth are C10–C11.
7. **C-7 Transport security.** LoomLink speaks HTTP to the address the user pairs with (LAN IP or Tailscale 100.x).
   Pairing: 8-char one-time code (31-symbol alphabet ≈ 39.6 bits) proven by HMAC-SHA256 over fresh nonces from both
   sides; per-device session key derived from code + nonces; every later request HMAC-signed over
   `METHOD|PATH|ts|sha256(body)` with a ±120 s window and strictly increasing ts. That gives authentication, integrity
   and replay protection, **not confidentiality** — Tailscale provides encryption; raw LAN payloads are readable.
   Android therefore allows cleartext app-wide (user-chosen IPs can't be enumerated in a network-security config).
   Known limit: the `hello` proof lets a LAN attacker brute-force the code offline; mitigated by one-time use, a
   **300 s code TTL** and rotation after 5 bad proofs / every pairing — a PAKE (e.g. SPAKE2) or TLS with a pinned
   desktop fingerprint is the fix (TODO). `last_ts` is not persisted, so after a desktop restart a captured request
   could be replayed within its 120 s window (TODO).
8. **C-8 QR = `loomlink://pair?u=<url>&c=<code>&n=<id>`** rendered by the desktop (`forge link serve`, terminal QR with
   the optional `qrcode` package). The phone does not embed a QR decoder: any camera/QR app opens the URI and Android
   routes it to PocketLoom's intent filter, which **pre-fills** the Link tab — the user must still tap Pair (no ambient
   authority).
9. **C-9 Capabilities** = `offload`, `sync` (granted by default), `drive_studio`, `mirror` (reserved for C9; never granted
   unless the desktop user passes `--allow`). Granted = requested ∩ allowed.
10. **C-10 Sync model.** Same on-disk store format both sides (`index.json` + `objects/aa/<sha>`), per-asset version
    vectors. Dominates → copy; equal → skip; concurrent with identical bytes → merge vectors on **both** sides;
    concurrent different bytes → **keep both**: desktop copy keeps the id, the phone copy becomes `<id>~<phone id>` on
    both sides (`conflict_of` recorded). Deletions are not synced yet (no tombstones) — TODO.
11. **C-11 Offload ops** accepted by the desktop today: `hashchain` (test/benchmark), `gen` (run one Content Forge brief),
    `recon.capture` (store only). LoomCluster (C7) will route these across nodes; until then the paired desktop runs them.
12. **C-12 Acceptance without a device.** No Android device and no KVM emulator on the build host, so the APK is accepted
    on static evidence (aapt/apksigner/lint/dex contents) + the **same FabricCore code** exercised on the JVM against the
    real desktop server. UI/intent behaviour on hardware is unverified and labelled so.
13. **C-13 Session key at rest** lives in app-private storage (`files/pocketloom/link/session.json`), excluded from cloud
    backup and device transfer; Android Keystore wrapping is a TODO.
14. **C-14 Versioning.** PocketLoom has its own `versionName` `0.1.0-c6`; the Fabric package became `0.7.1+f7` (patch
    bump — new `forge link` / `forge accept C*` commands; the Fabric milestone stays F7).
15. **C-15 Taken from `wip/f5-companions`:** the FabricCore draft (`Json`, `Store`, `Jobs`, `LocalForge`), the Gradle

## C7 / C8 — LoomCluster + GroundWeave (0.7.2+f7)

16. **C-16 [INFERENCE] Scope of the proof.** SPEC §9 describes a LAN/Tailscale fleet; the build host has one machine and
    CI one runner. C7 is therefore accepted on **loopback nodes (separate OS processes)**: protocol, scheduling,
    determinism and failure handling are real; transport across machines is not exercised.
17. **C-17 [INFERENCE] Node protocol.** HTTP/JSON (`/advert /hello /submit /steal /result /owner /status /results`),
    stdlib only, bound to `cluster.protocol.bind` (loopback). **No authentication/encryption** — the intended fix is to
    reuse LoomLink's per-device HMAC + capability model and run over Tailscale. Until then it must not be exposed.
18. **C-18 [INFERENCE] "Coordinator can be elected".** Every node schedules for itself; the "coordinator" (lowest live
    node id) is derived from adverts and used for reporting only — nothing depends on it, so there is no master.
19. **C-19 [INFERENCE] Execution semantics.** At-least-once with deterministic ops; first result wins. The origin keeps a
    lease per job (`lease_s` 6 s); ownership **moves with the job at steal time** (the draft told the origin only after
    the thief ran, which stranded work if the thief died in between — fixed and covered by the SIGKILL check).
20. **C-20 [INFERENCE] Retry.** A failing job is re-queued at the tail (stealable) up to `max_attempts` (3) executions, then
    reported failed with the last error. Value chosen to survive two transient faults; configurable in `forge.yaml`.
21. **C-21 [INFERENCE] Checkpointing = journal, not op snapshots.** Each node appends every queue/steal/result event to
    `journal.jsonl`; a restart re-queues held jobs and restores results (torn tail lines ignored). Ops are small and are
    re-run from scratch; no mid-op state is saved. Results for an unreachable origin are parked and redelivered.
22. **C-22 [INFERENCE] Allocation cap → slots.** `slots = max(1, cores × allocation_cap_pct / 100)`. Only CPU slots are
    enforced; RAM and GPU share caps from the spec are advertised/recorded but not enforced.
23. **C-23 [INFERENCE] Devices are names.** Adverts carry device names (`cpu`, `rocm`, `vulkan`, `directml`); a node with two
    GPUs that do not pool (Vega 64 + 56) advertises the backend once. Placement is "least-loaded capable node"; the
    acceptance run uses *simulated* adverts because there is no GPU to detect. Detection (`rocminfo`, `vulkaninfo`,
    Windows ⇒ `directml`) is best-effort and unverified on real GPU hardware.
24. **C-24 [INFERENCE] PocketLoom ↔ fleet.** Interop is via LoomLink: `LinkServer.cluster` (a `ClusterBridge`) turns each
    paired phone's `hashchain`/`gen` offload into a fleet job, round-robined over the entry node's mesh, blocking until the
    result returns (the phone's existing request/response shape); fleet unreachable ⇒ run on the desktop. A Kotlin
    *peer* node (phone advertising its own capacity and stealing work) is **not built**.
25. **C-25 [INFERENCE] GroundWeave ladder is data.** `content/groundweave/ladder.yaml` lists backends best-first per op;
    `impl: planned` rungs are documented intent and are never selected. Today every GPU rung is `planned`, so selection
    always lands on a CPU rung; `groundweave.execute` (runtime demotion on exception) exists for the first real GPU
    backend and is proven only on a synthetic ladder.
26. **C-26 [INFERENCE] Budgets and "GPU hidden".** `budget_s_2core` values (20–180 s) are regression gates set far above
    measured times, not performance targets. "2 cores" = `sched_setaffinity` to the first 2 CPUs + BLAS thread caps;
    "GPU hidden" = `HIP/ROCR/CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=-1` + `FORGE_GW_DEVICES=cpu` — on this GPU-less host that is true by
    construction, so the output is additionally required to be **byte-identical when every GPU is simulated present**.
27. **C-27 [INFERENCE] Probes for non-generator ops.** `runtime.girt` = the F3 headless run under the same pin (checks
    policy p95 ≤ the in-engine 500 µs budget, 0 fallbacks, no ONNX/GPU backend in the registry); `qa.balance` = a reduced
    12 × 2 matrix. The full 2400-fight G-BAL-01 run was not re-timed on 2 cores. `recon.splat` is `gpu_required`
    (3DGS has no honest CPU path) and is only *routed* (placement proven with a stand-in op); SplatForge is C12.
28. **C-28 [INFERENCE] Artifact transfer.** `gen` returns digests; with `return_data` it also returns base64 bytes so
    `forge cluster gen --out` can land them. Fine for current assets; large blobs need the LoomLink store/blob path.
29. **C-29 [INFERENCE] CI placement / version.** `forge accept C7-C8` runs in the parallel `pocketloom` job (needs Godot for
    the probes and the JDK/SDK for the Kotlin hop); pytest carries only `tests/test_c7_c8.py`. Version `0.7.2+f7` (patch:
    new commands/modules; milestone unchanged).
30. **C-30 [INFERENCE] Fleet manifest.** `forge.yaml` now lists all fleet nodes. Core counts: 5900X 12, Ryzen 5 1600 6,
    Dimensity 1080 8, i7-10510U 4, Nokia 7.2 **8** (the draft said 2 — corrected; the 2-core contract is a test pin, not that
    phone), Kindle Fire tablets listed with specs unknown and `allocation_cap_pct: 0` (dashboards only).
31. **C-31 Taken from `wip/f5-companions`:** `forge/loomcluster.py`, `forge/groundweave.py`, `forge/cli_cluster.py`,
    `content/groundweave/ladder.yaml`, the `cluster:` block of `forge.yaml` — reviewed, then changed as listed in
    CHANGELOG 0.7.2+f7 (steal ownership race, child-process ops, retry, redelivery, fleet harness, bridge, probes, `gen.world`,
    GroundWeave enforcement in the pipeline). Its `loomlink.py` was already superseded by `main`'s. `wip/f5-companions` is
    now fully consumed (its F5 game/balance files were superseded long ago) and can be deleted by the Producer.


## C9–C12 — MirrorWeave, LensForge, depth, SplatForge path (0.7.3+f7)

The spec (SPEC §4/§7/§8/§9/§13) names these four milestones but defines them only at feature-list level, and the task
was "real interfaces + clearly labelled stubs, a real CPU path only where cheap and spec-grounded". Everything below is
`[INFERENCE]` beyond that.

32. **C-32 What "accepted" means for C9–C12.** A milestone is accepted when its typed contract exists, every capability
    is registered in `content/companions/c9_c12.yaml` as `real`, `stub` or `deferred`, each stub *refuses* with a
    `[STUB <id> <Cn>]` message (never returns fake output), and the real CPU paths are tested. It does **not** mean the
    feature works: there is no live stream, no ARCore, no dense depth, no reconstruction. `forge.registry.check` (run by
    `forge accept C9-C12` and the tests) cross-checks the register against code, locale keys, Android strings, Kotlin symbols and the
    GroundWeave ladder so a stub cannot be silently promoted or a real entry silently removed.
33. **C-33 MirrorWeave wire.** Frames are signed LoomLink HTTP POSTs (`/mirror/frame`, `/mirror/latest`, …), one still or
    telemetry JSON per request, ≤ 8 MiB, strictly increasing `seq`, newest 8 kept per stream (`RING`), stream ids
    `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_-]{0,31}`. The spec lists WebSocket among the transports; no WebSocket/WebRTC is built — H.264/VP9/AV1
    chunks are a stub. Numbers are engineering defaults, not measurements.
34. **C-34 Control allow-list.** `drive_studio` permits exactly three read-only queries (`forge.status`,
    `groundweave.show`, `mirror.stats`). Input events to Studio, desktop→Android input and camera focus are stubs (501
    over the wire): Studio is itself a stub and Android input injection needs an AccessibilityService/MediaProjection
    consent flow that cannot be exercised here. Rationale: SPEC §8 "no ambient authority".
35. **C-35 Capabilities.** `mirror` (frames) and `drive_studio` (control) are the two capabilities C5 already reserved;
    neither is in the default grant (`offload`, `sync`) — the user opts in at pairing. Without the capability the route is
    403 (tested), unsigned is 401.
36. **C-36 Stub mechanism.** `StubError` (Python) / `StubException` (Kotlin) carry the capability id, milestone and a
    locale key (`stub.*` in `locales/en.json`, `stub_*` in Android `strings.xml`) — no hard-coded user-facing strings.
    The HTTP surface maps them to 501 with the same message.
37. **C-37 GroundWeave `planned` op class.** `depth.estimate`, `recon.mesh`, `shuttle.gdgs` are declared with
    `planned: true` and every rung `planned`: `depth.estimate` *could* run on CPU but is not built, so calling it
    `gpu_required` would be dishonest. `recon.splat` stays `gpu_required` + `route: loomcluster`; `shuttle.gdgs`
    has `route: local`. `mirror.encode` has a real CPU rung (stills/PNG pass-through + frame envelope); the hardware
    H.264 rung (AMF/VAAPI) is `planned`. New real CPU ops (`mirror.encode`, `lens.assess`, `depth.distance`,
    `recon.plan`) are timed on 2 pinned cores in `forge accept`.
38. **C-38 Capture profiles are advisory.** Mode names (`still, burst, orbit, sweep, video, depth_assist`), per-mode
    minimum views (1/3/24/40) and long edge (640/1280 px), and thresholds (sharpness ≥ 60, mean luma 40–215, clip fraction
    ≤ 5 %) are heuristics in `content/patternvault/capture_profiles.json` (the spec only says "capture modes" and
    "PatternVault capture profiles"). They have **not** been validated against a reconstruction run — none exists.
    Kotlin reads the same bundled file, so there is one source of truth.
39. **C-39 Quality metrics.** Gray = `(299R+587G+114B)//1000`; sharpness = variance of the 4-neighbour Laplacian over
    interior pixels; exposure = mean luma plus dark (≤ 5) / bright (≥ 250) clip fractions. Integer arithmetic so Python and
    Kotlin agree bit-for-bit (shared vectors `tests/fixtures/c9_c12_vectors.json`). Decoding: PNG with a built-in decoder;
    JPEG/WebP/EXIF need Pillow (optional `lens` extra, also in `dev`); a missing Pillow is reported, not faked.
40. **C-40 Overlap is not measured.** `assess_set` counts views, exact duplicates and best-of-burst; inter-view overlap /
    parallax would need feature matching and is reported as `"not_measured"`. A set can pass `assess_set` and still
    fail reconstruction.
41. **C-41 Depth.** Real: pinhole distance `d = f_px · real_size / pixel_size` with `f_px` from a 35 mm-equivalent focal
    length against a 36 mm frame width (or a direct pixel focal length), and least-squares scale/shift alignment of
    relative depth in inverse-depth space against ≥ 2 metric anchors (rejects degenerate/negative fits). The estimator that
    would *produce* relative depth (DepthAnything V2) is a stub.
42. **C-42 Depth model / ARCore licensing.** DepthAnything V2 weights have per-size licences (Small reported
    Apache-2.0, Base/Large/Giant reported CC-BY-NC-4.0) — **[unverified this run]; verify before bundling and run through
    BoltPack's licence audit.** ARCore (Android-only, Google SDK) is declared as an interface only and is not a dependency.
43. **C-43 RealityLoom route rule.** From SPEC §7: `backdrop` → 3DGS splat (SplatForge via `recon.splat`),
    `hero_prop` → photogrammetry (CPU/COLMAP) → MeshWeaver. Without a ROCm node the plan reports a *degraded* route; with
    one, only the placement check passes. No tool (COLMAP, OpenSplat, nerfstudio, Meshroom) is invoked or bundled; their
    presence is probed, never assumed.
44. **C-44 Capture bundle / plan.** `bundle.json` + sorted deterministic layout is our own format (no spec). The plan lists
    stages, `blocked_by` stub ids and an ETA *class* — always `unmeasured`; there are no timings. `runnable_today` is
    false for every target.
45. **C-45 `submit` semantics.** `forge recon submit` performs the real cluster placement check (needs a node advertising
    `rocm`), then raises the SplatForge stub. Nothing is queued, so a green placement check cannot be mistaken for a started job.
46. **C-46 3DGS `.ply` validator.** Validates the INRIA layout (binary_little_endian float32 vertex properties `x y z`,
    `f_dc_*`, `f_rest_*` (count ↔ SH degree 0–3), `opacity`, `scale_*`, `rot_*`). It checks a header + size, not render
    correctness. The `.splat`/compressed variants and a Godot GDGS importer are not done (`shuttle.gdgs` planned, local).
47. **C-47 PocketLoom/FabricCore.** FabricCore (pure Kotlin) gains `MirrorWeave`, `LensForge` (`CaptureModes`, `Quality`,
    `CaptureTelemetry`), `Depth`, `Recon` and `Stubs` with JUnit tests (35 in total) and CLI commands; the Android app only
    gains the `stub_*` strings. **No UI surfaces any of it and nothing ran on a phone** (no device/KVM). Kotlin stub
    interfaces (`MediaProjectionSource`, `AndroidInputInjector`, `FocusController`, `ARCoreDepth`) are declarations that throw.
48. **C-48 Pillow.** Added as an *optional* dev/`lens` dependency only (HPND-style licence; add to BoltPack/THIRD_PARTY
    review if it ever ships in a build). Core runtime and CI-critical paths work without it.
49. **C-49 Version / CI.** Patch bump `0.7.2+f7` → `0.7.3+f7` (additive, offset unchanged). CI adds one step
    (`forge accept C9-C12 --no-write`) to the parallel `pocketloom` job; pytest gains `tests/test_c9_c12.py`.
50. **C-50 After C12.** SPEC §13's companion table ends at C12. The next *spec* items are F8 (retro/console-style, optional
    — confirm scope with the Producer first) and F9 (post, LiveOps readiness only). We have not started F8 and its scope
    is not assumed. Non-spec follow-ups (a real GPU rung, LAN/Tailscale cluster with node auth, on-device run) are in TODO.md.
